# LRSSS (CQLR c. R-22.1): scope summary

Checked on LégisQuébec (per-section version pages, consolidation current to 2026-10-08). Most of the Act has been in force since 2024-07-01. Sections 3, 4, 38, 39, 77 and the Schedules were reworded on 2024-12-01 when Santé Québec was created (2023, c. 34).

**Who is bound (s. 4).** The opening words are "est un organisme du secteur de la santé et des services sociaux :", followed by:
- (1°) the Ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux;
- (2°) "une personne ou un groupement visé à l'annexe I ou à l'annexe II";
- (3°) "Santé Québec, un établissement autre qu'un établissement de Santé Québec" and the Nunavik regional board;
- (4°) a person or group that provides services on behalf of a body in 2° or 3°, but only for those activities (s. 4, para. 2);
- (5°) any other person or group designated by government regulation. R-22.1, r. 1, s. 1 adds college and university institutions for the health services they provide.

Under s. 4, para. 3, an *intervenant* working inside a body other than Santé Québec or an establishment is treated as a body if that body does not keep the intervenant's records.

- **Schedule I** lists public bodies: Commissaire à la santé et au bien-être, Commission sur les soins de fin de vie, Urgences-santé, Héma-Québec, INESSS, INSPQ and RAMQ. Item 8 (the organ-donation coordinator) is not in force.
- **Schedule II** lists 11 private-sector categories. These include item 1, "une personne ou un groupement qui exploite un cabinet privé de professionnel", and item 2, "une personne ou un groupement qui exploite un centre médical spécialisé", both "au sens de la Loi sur la gouvernance du système de santé et de services sociaux" (G-1.021). The others are assisted-procreation centres, laboratories, private seniors' residences, intermediate and family-type resources, lodging resources, funeral-services permit holders, ambulance-service permit holders, palliative-care homes and the health communication centre.

**Answer for private medical centres and private clinics: YES, the Act applies.**
- A private clinic or medical office is a *cabinet privé de professionnel*, and is covered by Schedule II, item 1 through s. 4(2°).
- A *centre médical spécialisé* is covered by Schedule II, item 2 through s. 4(2°).
- A private health institution holding an *établissement* permit, such as a private CHSLD (conventionné or not), is an *établissement* under G-1.021. It is covered by s. 4(3°) ("un établissement autre qu'un établissement de Santé Québec"), with the definition in s. 3.
- A private provider acting under contract for a covered body is covered by s. 4(4°) for those services only.

Schedule II bodies get some lighter obligations under R-22.1, r. 2:
- s. 7: access analysis is annual instead of monthly;
- s. 8: no governance committee is required.

**Displacement of the private-sector Act (P-39.1).** P-39.1, s. 3, para. 3° (added by 2023, c. 5, s. 229, in force 2024-07-01) says the private-sector Act does not apply "aux renseignements de santé et de services sociaux au sens de la Loi sur les renseignements de santé … (chapitre R-22.1) détenus par un organisme du secteur de la santé et des services sociaux au sens de cette loi ou par une personne autre qu'un tel organisme, pour le compte de ce dernier".

As a result, for health information held by a private clinic or medical centre, the R-22.1 equivalents replace the P-39.1 obligations, including:
- person in charge and governance policies (P-39.1 ss. 3.1–3.2);
- privacy impact assessments (s. 3.3);
- confidentiality incidents and their register (ss. 3.5–3.8);
- communication outside Québec (s. 17);
- the P-39.1 administrative monetary penalty regime.

P-39.1 still applies to other personal information the clinic holds, such as employee and HR files and marketing contacts. This is an inference from the wording of s. 3, para. 3°.

**Penalties.** Chapter X of R-22.1 sets penal fines only:
- s. 159: $1,000–$10,000 for a natural person, $3,000–$30,000 otherwise;
- s. 160: $5,000–$100,000 for a natural person, $15,000–$150,000 otherwise;
- s. 161: fines doubled or tripled for repeat offences;
- s. 162: fines doubled for directors and officers.

None of the sections checked creates *sanctions administratives pécuniaires*. The Commission's tools are inspection (s. 115), peremptory demands (s. 122) and orders after investigation (s. 124).
