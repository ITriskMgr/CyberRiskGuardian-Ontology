The CyberRiskGuardian Ontology (CRGO) is a formal vocabulary for cybersecurity risk assessment. It was built to give a precise, machine-checkable meaning to the data produced by CyberRiskGuardian, an AI-assisted risk assessment method and toolkit, and to support the identification of threats, vulnerabilities and risk scenarios for an organisation.

Design. CRGO is an OWL 2 DL ontology aligned with Basic Formal Ontology (BFO-2020, ISO/IEC 21838-2) and an extract of the Common Core Ontologies. It is split into seventeen modules: crgo-gov (governance annotations), crgo-core (assets, threats, weaknesses, scenarios, controls, frameworks, assessment and treatment), crgo-measure (method-independent estimates), crgo-crg (the CyberRiskGuardian quantitative model); crgo-evidence, crgo-org, crgo-vuln, crgo-grc and crgo-mapping (sources and observations, organisation profile, vulnerability observations, legal applicability and compliance, typed and qualified correspondences); crgo-governance, crgo-assurance, crgo-audit and crgo-incident (governance, assurance, the audit lifecycle, incidents); and, from 0.10.0, crgo-policy, crgo-compliance, crgo-siem and crgo-soar (policy and compliance management, security monitoring and detection, orchestration and response). Every term has English and French labels and definitions, and carries its source, confidence, governance status, approver and the release that introduced it. A risk scenario is a description of what might happen, not an event; its parts (threat source, threat event, weakness, consequence) are descriptions too, so that generic library scenarios can be recorded without inventing an organisation.

Around the ontology. SKOS vocabularies cover NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, ISO/IEC 27002:2022 (identifiers and titles), COBIT 2019, MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise, Québec Law 25, the Québec Act respecting health and social services information and the domains of the CSA Cloud Controls Matrix, the EU GDPR, NIST SP 800-53A, audit and assurance standards (identifiers and paraphrases), ATT&CK detection strategies and analytics, and OCSF event classes. A library of about 1,900 generic risk scenarios is linked to them, with a confidence and an approval status on every link, and with calibration estimates from a 2023 scenario generator and from teaching cases. crgo-full imports everything.

Use. Load crgo to model assessments, or crgo-full for the library and vocabularies. CyberRiskGuardian uses CRGO through a local MCP server and a JSON export (see the repository's docs/crg-integration.md). Library and case values are calibration priors, never organisational evidence.
