Abstract

The CyberRiskGuardian Ontology (CRGO) gives a formal, reasoner-checked meaning to cybersecurity risk assessment data: assets, threat sources and events, vulnerabilities, risk scenarios, controls, frameworks and legal obligations, the CyberRiskGuardian quantitative model and the key risk indicators computed from it. It is published with SKOS vocabularies for NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53, ISO/IEC 27002:2022, COBIT 2019, MITRE ATT&CK, Québec Law 25 and the Québec health-information act, and with a library of about 1,900 generic risk scenarios linked to them.

Introduction back to ToC

The CyberRiskGuardian Ontology (CRGO) is a formal vocabulary for cybersecurity risk assessment. It was built to give a precise, machine-checkable meaning to the data produced by CyberRiskGuardian, an AI-assisted risk assessment method and toolkit, and to support the identification of threats, vulnerabilities and risk scenarios for an organisation.

Design. CRGO is an OWL 2 DL ontology aligned with Basic Formal Ontology (BFO-2020, ISO/IEC 21838-2) and an extract of the Common Core Ontologies. It is split into four modules: crgo-gov (governance annotations), crgo-core (assets, threats, weaknesses, scenarios, controls, frameworks, assessment and treatment), crgo-measure (method-independent estimates) and crgo-crg (the CyberRiskGuardian quantitative model). Every term has English and French labels and definitions, and carries its source, confidence, governance status, approver and the release that introduced it. A risk scenario is a description of what might happen, not an event; its parts (threat source, threat event, weakness, consequence) are descriptions too, so that generic library scenarios can be recorded without inventing an organisation.

Around the ontology. SKOS vocabularies cover NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, ISO/IEC 27002:2022 (identifiers and titles), COBIT 2019, MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise, Québec Law 25, the Québec Act respecting health and social services information and the domains of the CSA Cloud Controls Matrix. A library of about 1,900 generic risk scenarios is linked to them, with a confidence and an approval status on every link, and with calibration estimates from a 2023 scenario generator and from teaching cases. crgo-full imports everything.

Use. Load crgo to model assessments, or crgo-full for the library and vocabularies. CyberRiskGuardian uses CRGO through a local MCP server and a JSON export (see the repository's docs/crg-integration.md). Library and case values are calibration priors, never organisational evidence.

Each element carries its own governance status; new elements stay proposed until the domain expert approves them.
